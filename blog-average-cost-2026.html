Average wedding cost in 2026: what the numbers can tell you

DL
Written by Dan. Liu / Wedding Budget Planner
Updated October 4, 2026

A national average can give you context. It cannot tell you what a venue in your area will charge or how much you can afford. Use it to frame questions, then build your budget from local quotes.

Your starting checklist

  • Check the country, wedding year, sample, and what the survey includes.
  • Distinguish completed wedding costs from budgets for weddings still being planned.
  • Compare a similar guest count and event type before drawing conclusions.
  • Set your own ceiling and collect local quotes before using an average as a target.

The Knot's 2026 study reports $34,200 for U.S. weddings held in 2025, based on 10,474 couples. Zola's 2025 First Look report gives $36,000 from more than 6,000 couples planning 2025 weddings.

The $1,800 difference is not evidence that costs rose by that amount. The reports use different samples and stages of planning. Neither figure is a universal price for weddings held in 2026.

The source-check download records the report year, wedding year, sample, and scope so you can compare them without mixing those details.

How does guest count affect the average?

The Knot reports an average of 117 guests and $292 per guest. Its guest-count bands give more context:

Do not multiply a survey's per-guest figure and expect a reliable quote. Fixed venue and photography costs, minimum spends, service levels, and capacity changes can make the relationship uneven. See the 50 vs 100 guests example for a smaller calculation you can check against your vendors.

In the same Knot study, hometown weddings average $32,600, domestic destination weddings $41,700, and international destination weddings $43,700. These groups are not like-for-like quotes for your plans.

Ask local providers about your date, access hours, menu, staffing, rentals, tax, and travel. Decide which guests' accommodation or transport you plan to pay for rather than assuming every survey treats it the same way.

What if our ceiling is below the average?

Keep the ceiling based on money you can actually use. A few expensive weddings can pull an arithmetic average upward; the number is not an instruction to spend more.

Price the essentials first, then compare a smaller list, different date, simpler meal, or another venue. Keep the choices you care about and make specific trade-offs elsewhere. The spend-or-save guide shows how to compare an upgrade with the reserve it would use.
