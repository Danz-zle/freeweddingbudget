Wedding venue cost checklist before you sign

DL
Written by Dan. Liu / Wedding Budget Planner
Updated October 4, 2026

An inexpensive room can need expensive additions. Bring the same event brief to every visit so you can compare the space, required services, and costs still missing.

Your starting checklist

  • Use the same date, guest count, rooms, access hours, and reception plan.
  • List furniture, staff, security, cleaning, mandatory vendors, minimum spends, tax, and overtime.
  • Walk the guest and delivery routes; ask about capacity, accessibility, parking, power, noise, and weather backup.
  • Take the payment and cancellation terms home to read before paying.

A $2,500 room reaches $6,200 before tax

These fictional USD quotes are for a 100-guest event. Venue B adds $1,200 furniture, $500 security, $600 cleanup, and a $1,400 tent allowance: $2,500 + $1,200 + $500 + $600 + $1,400 = $6,200.

Venue A's security arrangements are unconfirmed. Its $4,000 figure is therefore a listed subtotal, not proof it provides the cheapest complete event. Tax, access hours, catering rules, and contract terms still need checking for every venue. Venue B's tent allowance is a cost placeholder, not confirmation of a safe weather shelter.

  1. Add comparable venue quotes in Vendors. Include required staffing, cleaning, security, furniture, backup arrangements, overtime, and tax where confirmed.

  3. Review Venue and Catering in Budget, select the commitments, then put their actual contract dates and amounts in Payments.

Will guests and suppliers be able to use the space?

Walk from parking or drop-off through the ceremony, meal, restrooms, and exits. Check step-free routes, elevators, seating, assistance procedures, shuttle access, and the weather-backup layout. Suppliers also need loading access, power, and time to work.

Ask the venue which legal requirements apply. For U.S. venues, the ADA Standards for Accessible Design are a starting reference, not a substitute for an accessibility assessment of your event.

What is the weather or disruption plan?

Confirm the alternative location, decision deadline, responsible person, official alert source, communication plan, and any extra cost. Ask the venue about safe shelter or evacuation for the conditions you could face; a promise to "work something out" is not a plan.

Which terms should be in writing?
  • Date, spaces, capacity, access and end times, full price, required charges, tax, payment dates, and damage deposit conditions.
  • Included furniture, staff, cleaning, parking, equipment, and dressing rooms.
  • Required or prohibited suppliers, catering and alcohol rules, and minimum spend.
  • Setup, teardown, noise, candles, confetti, power, damage, and overtime approval.
  • Cancellation, refunds, rescheduling, weather, force majeure, insurance, permits, and client responsibilities.
  • Accessible routes, emergency arrangements, floor plan, and written backup plan.

Keep contracts, invoices, receipts, insurance documents, plans, and approved changes. The FTC's recordkeeping guidance explains why copies and communication records matter. Check local advice for uncertain contract rights; do not assume an automatic cooling-off period.

Does it fit our category limit?

Against the original $11,000 Venue example, listed subtotals of $4,000, $6,200, and $5,800 leave room on paper. Your limit may differ, and omitted security or other charges still have to be priced.

Allow for ceremony fees, permits, transport, insurance, rentals, accessibility arrangements, and overtime before moving the remainder to upgrades. Location, guest comfort, catering restrictions, and personal fit matter as well as price.
