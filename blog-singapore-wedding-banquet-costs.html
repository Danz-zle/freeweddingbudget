Singapore wedding banquet costs: what ++ really adds, from eight hotel price lists

DL
Written by Dan Liu
Published November 6, 2026

Singapore hotels quote a wedding banquet per table of ten, or per person, followed by "++". On the five per-table price lists below, the headline runs from S$1,638 to S$2,288 per table, but the ++ adds a 10% service charge and then GST, which is about 20% more than the headline if GST is 9%, and the minimum number of tables ranges from 8 to 30. This guide shows what each hotel published, what the ++ does to the arithmetic, which extras are priced separately, and what to ask before you compare any two.

What ++ adds to a headline price

In these documents, "++" means two charges are added after the printed price. The Hilton document says ++ is a 10% service charge and prevailing GST. Amara's says prices are subject to a 10% service charge, then prevailing government taxes, without naming the tax. The Andaz document is the only one that states a rate, at 9% GST. This guide uses 9% for its examples and flags it as an assumption wherever another document is concerned; the order of the charges is also assumed to be the service charge first, then GST on the total.

So the S$1,888 headline is S$2,263.71 per table once both charges are added, which is S$375.71, or 19.9%, more. Across a banquet of 20 tables, that is S$7,514.24 beyond the headline subtotal of S$37,760. Whenever a price list shows ++, multiply by 1.10 and then by the GST rate before you compare it with anything else.

The floor decides which hall you can book

The table count in a price list is not a detail. It is the rule that decides which room, and sometimes which price, is open to you. Hilton's document sets different ranges for each room and session. A banquet of 20 tables fits its Imperial Ballroom but not its Grand Ballroom, which starts at 26 tables for lunch, 28 for dinner and 30 on Saturday. Amara's package needs 20 tables in its pillarless ballroom, Marriott Tang Plaza's needs 20 tables or 200 guests, and the document filed as Wedding Dinner Package 2026 gives 18 to 26 tables in its Balestier Ballroom and 10 to 12 in a smaller room. Holiday Inn Singapore Atrium has the lowest floor of the five, at 8 tables for its ballroom banquet.

If your guest list is below a hall's minimum, you have three choices: invite more people, pay as if you had, or use a smaller room. Ask for the smaller room's price before you assume the large hall's price applies, because the Hilton table below keeps the same price per table in both rooms for lunch and Monday to Thursday dinner.

Questions to put in writing

  • Does the ++ mean a 10% service charge and then GST, and at what GST rate?
  • Is "nett" an all-in price, and if so what does it include?
  • What are the minimum tables or guests for my date, hall and session, and what happens if fewer attend?
  • Which menu, with how many courses, does this price cover?
  • Which extras, such as beer, wine, corkage and upgrades, are charged separately?
  • What are the deposit, payment schedule and cancellation terms, and until when is this price valid?

What these price lists cannot tell you

They are published list prices, not negotiated or paid prices. Only two of the eight documents sit on a hotel's own site, none prints a document date, and the copies hosted on wedding listing sites may be out of date. Several do not name the hotel inside the document, the Marriott Tang Plaza document prints only "$" without naming the currency (assumed here to be Singapore dollars), and the 9% GST rate comes from one document. All eight are hotels. This guide did not find current published price lists for Chinese restaurants, so it says nothing about them, and it covers no venue outside hotels. It gives general information, not financial advice, and describes no personal experience. If you are paying from another currency, the guide on budgeting in two currencies converts S$ banquet totals to US dollars at a dated reference rate and shows what a rate move and card fees can add.
