Guest count and cost

How much is a wedding for 100 people? What The Knot and Zola publish

DL
Written by Dan Liu
Published November 19, 2026

There is no single published price for a 100-guest wedding. Two US wedding surveys, The Knot's and Zola's, give answers that start about $28,000 apart, and none of them is a quote. The Knot's 2026 Real Weddings Study puts weddings of 51 to 100 guests at an average of $27,200 and weddings of more than 100 guests at $43,300. Zola's 2026 pages suggest $150 to $300 per guest, which is $15,000 to $30,000 for 100 people. This guide sets each figure out with its source and date, shows the arithmetic, and explains why you should not treat any of them as your own total.

The short answer

  • For 100 guests, published reference points run from $15,000 to $43,300. The spread comes from what each number measures, not from an error in one of them.
  • A guest count of exactly 100 sits on the edge of The Knot's brackets: 100 guests falls in "51 to 100", while 101 falls in "over 100", and those two averages differ by $16,100.
  • Averages describe groups of couples who married in 2025 or booked in 2026. Your total depends on the venue, the region, the date and which vendors you hire, so use these figures to check a quote, not to replace one.

For context, The Knot's bracket for weddings of 1 to 50 guests is $17,100. Its description of the study says the figures cover the ceremony and reception and do not include the engagement ring, the rehearsal dinner or the honeymoon, which it lists separately.

The jump from $27,200 to $43,300 is not what one extra guest costs. The brackets are averages of different groups of couples, and couples with larger guest lists may also choose different venues, more vendors and longer events. Read the two numbers as a range, not as a price list.

Two things stand out. First, the sources disagree on single lines by thousands of dollars: The Knot's venue average is $4,327 above Zola's, while Zola's bar average is $2,742 above The Knot's alcohol figure. Second, the eleven lines added together come to $38,910 for The Knot and $43,136 for Zola, both above the headline averages of $34,200 and $36,000. Neither page explains why. One possible reason is that a category average describes only couples who hired that vendor, so adding every line overstates a typical wedding. The pages do not say whether this is the case, so treat the sums as a way to see the gap, not as a budget.

Why the numbers disagree, and how to read them

Different guest counts. The Knot reports an average of 117 guests. In a separate Zola article, its First Look Report is quoted as putting the average guest list at 145 people. Dividing each source's headline average by its own guest count gives about $292 per guest for The Knot and about $248 for Zola. The same sum on two data sets produces two answers, so a per-guest figure is a rule of thumb, not a rate.

Different surveys. The Knot's study surveyed 10,474 US couples who married in 2025. Zola's figures come from its own 2026 reports and cost index. Their pools of couples, the way they ask about costs and the places couples live will not match.

Fixed and per-guest costs. Catering, drinks, rentals and invitations rise with the guest list. A photographer's package, an officiant or a DJ usually does not. Multiplying a per-guest average by your headcount treats everything as per-guest, so it can misstate a total when much of the budget does not scale. If you want to see the catering side of that sum with real fee rules, the catering fee guide applies five published 2026 price lists to a round $100 menu for 100 guests and gets between $12,300 and $13,926.40 before drinks. That is an illustration, not a quote, and it sits above The Knot's $8,000 catering average because the venues add service charges and tax.

Averages are not medians. An average can be pulled up by a few very expensive weddings. The pages I read do not give a median, so I cannot say what the typical couple spent.

Turn the ranges into your own number

  1. Start with your list. Write down the number of adults, children and vendors who will be fed. A published bracket is for the whole group, not for adults alone.
  2. Split guest-driven from fixed lines. Catering, drinks, rentals, stationery and favors move with headcount. Photography, music and the officiant mostly do not.
  3. Get one real quote for the biggest lines. Venue and catering are the two largest averages in both sources, so a written quote for each tells you more than any national figure.
  4. Compare your total with the range, not a single number. If your total is far below $27,200 or above $43,300, ask what your region, date or style explains. Neither is automatically wrong.
  5. Keep a buffer for lines you have not priced. Percentage service charges, tax and minimums are covered in the guides on hidden wedding costs and 50 versus 100 guests.

When you have real quotes, enter them in the Planner so the total sits beside the rest of your budget.

What these figures cannot tell you

They are published averages, not quotes and not what any one couple paid. The multiplications and sums marked "my arithmetic" are mine, not the sources'. I read the four pages listed below on November 19, 2026, and quote the figures as the pages showed them that day; pages can change after that. The Knot figures cover US couples married in 2025, so they say nothing about your country or city. Zola's per-guest range is printed as advice for "a typical wedding", and the page says it varies by location and service level. This is general information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Check every figure with your own vendors before you rely on it.
