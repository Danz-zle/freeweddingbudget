Wedding catering for 50 guests: where minimums and fees show up in five 2026 price lists

DL
Written by Dan Liu
Published November 26, 2026

Fifty is a number that appears again and again in published catering price lists. Some caterers set a 50-guest minimum for a package, a Saturday, or a single station. Others print percentage charges of 18% to 26% on top of the menu price. This guide reads five 2026 price lists, shows exactly where 50 appears in each, and works out what 50 guests of a printed price becomes. It does not tell you what your caterer will charge. It shows what to ask.

The short answer

  • A list of exactly 50 clears the minimums printed in all five documents, but a list of 48 may not. Ask each caterer what happens below its number, because most of these documents do not say.
  • In three of the five lists a percentage charge of 18%, 23% or 26% is added to the printed price before tax. In the other two the printed price already includes service, at least as the document words it.
  • Small add-ons are charged per head or per attendant. At 50 guests a $150-an-hour chef attendant costs more per guest than at 75, because one attendant is needed either way.

The taxes the documents do print. Waterside Inn says its prices are subject to 18% gratuity and 13% HST. It does not say whether the HST applies to the gratuity. If HST applies to the package price only, the Lakeshore total for 50 is $11,069.50. If it applies to the package price plus gratuity, it is $11,267.23. That is a $197.73 difference on one quote, so ask. The Sea Pines and Morton Arboretum documents mention state tax, but do not print a rate. Their totals above are before tax.

Hampshire House prints two things worth reading together. The package price is described as including gratuity, administrative fee and taxes, and another line says the amount applied to the food and beverage minimum is $173.60 per person. At 50 guests that is $8,680.00 (my arithmetic). The difference from the $230 price is $56.40 per person, or $2,820.00 for 50. The document does not say what that difference covers. If a price list prints two per-person numbers, ask which one is the price and which one counts toward the room minimum.

Extras that are charged per head or per attendant

A few lines in these documents behave differently at 50 guests than at 100. They are small, but they are the lines that quietly move a quote.

  • Chef attendants. Sea Pines requires one chef attendant per 75 guests at $150 an hour for carving and action stations, based on 90 minutes of service. That is $225 per attendant (my arithmetic: $150 × 1.5 hours). With 50 guests you still need one attendant, so the cost is $4.50 per guest. With 75 guests it is $3.00 per guest. The document does not say how it counts 76 guests, so ask.
  • Choosing more than one entrée. Sea Pines prints a $10 per guest charge for its multiple-entrée option, which allows up to three entrées. Morton Arboretum prints $5 per guest when more than one entrée is chosen, and says it applies to all guests. At 50 guests those are $500 and $250 (my arithmetic). Neither document says whether the percentage charge applies on top.
  • Passed hors d'oeuvres. Sea Pines says its five passed hors d'oeuvres are not included in dinner package pricing and prints $60 per guest for them. At 50 guests that is $3,000. Added to the $98 entrée, the subtotal becomes $158 per guest, or $7,900 for 50, and with the 26% service charge $9,954.00 before tax (my arithmetic).
  • Upgraded drinks. Morton Arboretum lists its Prestige Brands liquor package as an additional $18 per guest. For 50 guests that is $900 (my arithmetic), before its 23% charge and tax if they apply to it.

How to read a price list at 50 guests

Plus-plus or all-in. Hampshire House and McMaster print prices that include gratuity or service. Waterside Inn, Sea Pines and Morton Arboretum print prices before percentage charges. Neither style is better, but you cannot compare $230 with $169 until you put them on the same footing. For Waterside's $169, adding 18% gives $199.42 before HST.

The charge you cannot see on the menu page. Morton Arboretum prints its 23% charge after several menu sections rather than beside each price. Sea Pines repeats its 26% line beneath several package sections. If you only read the headline price you will miss it, so search each document for the words "service", "administrative" and "gratuity".

Minimums are not the same as guarantees. A 50-guest minimum may apply to a package, a day of the week, a season or a single station. Waterside Inn's applies to Saturday ballroom weddings from May to October. Ask whether it applies to your date, not just to the caterer.

Questions to ask before you sign for 50

  1. Is the price you quoted before or after service charges, gratuity and tax? Ask for the percentage and the amount you will actually pay at 50 guests.
  2. What is the minimum, and what does it apply to? Guests, a package, a station, a day or a total spend? Ask what you pay if your count lands below it.
  3. Which charges are per guest, and which are per attendant or per station? Ask how many chefs, bartenders and servers are required at 50 and at 60.
  4. What does choosing a second entrée cost? Ask whether the fee applies to all guests and whether the percentage charge applies to the fee.
  5. When is the final guest count due and what does it lock in? A count of 50 that drops to 46 may still be billed at the minimum.

When you have written quotes, enter the confirmed totals in the Planner so catering sits next to the rest of your budget. For the 100-guest version of the same fee arithmetic, see the catering fee guide, and for how a larger or smaller list moves the whole budget see 50 versus 100 guests.

What this guide cannot tell you

These are five documents from five venues: two in Ontario, Canada, and three in the United States. They do not describe your caterer, your region or your date. Only one of them, the Morton Arboretum menu, prints a revision date (August 2026); the other four have no issue date printed, and one says its prices are subject to change after December 2026, so I cannot say how current each is. Waterside Inn and McMaster print prices with a dollar sign but no currency name, and their HST suggests Canadian dollars, which I did not convert. The totals marked "my arithmetic" are mine, not the venues', and any venue may apply its charges differently from the way I applied them. I read all five documents on November 26, 2026, and quote the figures as they appeared that day; documents can change after that. This is general information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Confirm every figure with your caterer in writing.
