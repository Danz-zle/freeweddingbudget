DIY Wedding Decor That Looks Professional

DL
Written by Dan. Liu / Wedding Budget Planner
Practical budgeting guide • Updated June 2026

DIY decor can save money, but only when the projects are simple, repeatable, and finished before the wedding day. The goal is not to copy a luxury florist. The goal is to make the room feel intentional without adding stress, transport problems, or last-minute setup work.

Examples use USD for simplicity. You can adapt the calculator to your local currency and vendor quotes. Flower prices, candle rules, venue setup time, and rental costs vary by location.

Choose projects that scale

A good DIY project can be repeated across tables without needing special tools. Good examples include table numbers, printed signs, bud vases, candle groupings, welcome table details, and simple photo displays. Riskier projects include ceiling installations, large floral arches, heavy backdrops, and anything that requires ladders on the wedding morning.

Sample decor scenario

For a 10 table reception, a couple might budget $400 for candles, $300 for small vases, $250 for greenery or simple stems, $150 for printed signage, and $200 for backup supplies. That $1,300 example can feel polished if the colors and materials repeat. It can also fail if setup takes three hours and no one is assigned to do it.

Checklist

  • Ask the venue whether open flame is allowed.
  • Confirm who can access the room and when.
  • Test one table before buying supplies for every table.
  • Use containers that are easy to pack and transport.
  • Assign setup and cleanup to someone other than the couple.

Common mistakes

The biggest mistake is choosing projects because they look good online but are hard to repeat. Another mistake is buying supplies without checking table size. A centerpiece that looks nice at home may be too wide once plates, glasses, favors, and menus are added.

How to use the calculator

Use the wedding budget calculator to separate decor into supplies, rentals, flowers, delivery, and backup items. If the decor budget grows too large, compare it with where to spend and save before buying more supplies.
