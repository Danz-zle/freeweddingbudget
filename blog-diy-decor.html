DIY wedding decor: compare the full cost before buying

DL
Written by Dan. Liu / Wedding Budget Planner
Updated October 4, 2026

Making decorations can save money or give you something personal. Price the supplies, transport, setup, and cleanup before deciding whether it is the easier option.

Your starting checklist

  • Make one complete sample before buying supplies for every table.
  • Include tools, printing, storage, transport, breakages, and cleanup.
  • Confirm who will set everything up and remove it within the venue's access hours.
  • Compare the same finished result with a rental or florist quote, and check venue safety rules.

A $600 vase-and-flower idea becomes a $1,200 project

In this fictional USD example, vases and flowers cost $600. LED candles, printing, tools, transport, and a backup allowance bring the total to $1,200 before helper time.

These prices are fictional, not local quotes. Check that each option covers the same flowers, containers, styling, delivery, setup, pickup, and cleanup. The rental description does not confirm flowers, and the DIY total does not price helper time. Tax and omitted services could change the comparison.

Ask whether a supplier handles replacements and last-minute problems. If you choose DIY, agree who handles them when you are getting ready for the wedding.

Which projects should we attempt ourselves?

Repeatable tabletopBud vases, low greenery, flameless candle groupsQuantity, table size, sightlines, water, and breakageBuild one complete table before purchasing all units.
Time-sensitiveFresh flowers, fruit, food-based favors, ice, live plantsStorage, temperature, water, freshness, and disposalConfirm who prepares and moves them close to the event.
Higher complexityLarge arches, suspended decor, powered lighting, heavy backdropsStructural support, electricity, height, installation, and liabilityUse a qualified provider and confirm the venue approves the installation.

Printed signs, table numbers, and a tested table arrangement can be easier to repeat than fresh-flower installations. Fresh flowers add storage, temperature, and timing demands. Leave structural hanging, complex electrical work, and work requiring specialist equipment to qualified providers.

Pack by table or location, label boxes, and photograph the sample setup. Confirm access times and who takes tools, packaging, rentals, and leftover decorations home. A low materials bill can still require several people working while other wedding tasks are underway.

What safety checks matter?

Ask the venue about candles, attachments, power, exits, and cleanup restrictions before purchasing. The U.S. Fire Administration recommends flameless candles and stable holders for lit candles. Venue rules may be stricter.

Check cords for damage and do not overload them; see the CPSC's extension-cord safety guidance. Keep cables and decorations out of walking routes. Discuss accessible routes with the venue; the ADA primer for U.S. businesses provides background on accessibility duties.
