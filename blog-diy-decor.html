DIY Wedding Decor That Looks Professional (Without Breaking Your Budget)

SM
Sarah Mitchell Wedding Planning Expert

The floral and decor budget for a typical 150-guest wedding runs $4,000 to $7,000 when handled by professionals. That includes ceremony arrangements, reception centerpieces, bridal bouquet, bridesmaid flowers, boutonnieres, and accent pieces. For couples with more creativity than cash, a strategic DIY approach can cut this to $1,500-$2,500 while still looking stunning in person and in photos.

DIY Floral Centerpieces

Professional centerpieces average $75-$150 each. DIY versions using grocery store flowers and greenery cost $15-$30 each. The secret is choosing flowers that look expensive but are not: carnations (when clustered tightly), baby's breath, eucalyptus, and seasonal blooms from wholesale flower markets.

  • Buy flowers 2 days before the wedding, not earlier. Most grocery store flowers last 5-7 days.
  • Use bulk-bought clear glass cylinder vases and floating candles to add high-end ambient light at low costs.

Additionally, focus on structural greenery like Italian Ruscus or Silver Dollar Eucalyptus. Greenery provides a lush, natural aesthetic that covers a large surface area for a fraction of the cost of premium focal florals like garden roses or peonies. Assemble centerpieces in shallow trays or wide vases using floral foam blocks to help them hold their shape and stay hydrated overnight.

The Power of Cohesive Candlelight

When it comes to creating a premium atmosphere on a budget, candlelight is your best friend. Warm flickering candlelight masks minor design imperfections and automatically lends a high-end, romantic glow to any room. Buy votives and glass cylinder holders in bulk online, and space them generously down long reception tables. Combine taper candles, pillars, and small tea lights to add visual height and depth without needing expensive floral installations.

Elegant Digital and Foam Board Signage

Custom-painted wooden or acrylic signs look beautiful, but paying designer fees for multiple signs can easily cost hundreds of dollars. Instead, use free design templates online to curate a cohesive, professional suite of signage. Print your welcome signs, seating charts, and bar menus at a local print shop on sturdy foam core boards. Display them on simple easels decorated with leftover greenery branches to maintain a polished, professional look.

Important: What NOT to DIY

While DIY can save you major money, some projects are best left to licensed professionals to prevent massive day-of stress. Never attempt to DIY complicated ceiling drapery, complex lighting rigs, or large-scale floral hanging installations. These require special rigging, safety checks, and time-intensive labor that will exhaust you or your family members on the morning of the wedding. Keep your DIY efforts focused on stable tabletop items that can be pre-assembled days in advance.
