How to use this wedding budget planner

This planner is designed for couples who want to test wedding costs before committing to deposits, vendor balances, and guest-count decisions. Start with a total budget, adjust your guest count, review the category split, then add real quotes or expenses as planning becomes more detailed.

Step What to do Why it helps
1. Set your total budget Enter the maximum amount you are comfortable spending. This creates a spending ceiling before vendor quotes start changing the plan.
2. Adjust guest count Add family, friends, colleagues, and other guests. Guest count affects catering, drinks, rentals, favors, and sometimes venue size.
3. Review categories Compare venue, catering, photography, decor, attire, and other costs. This shows where the largest trade-offs usually happen.
4. Add real expenses Record deposits, vendor quotes, and paid balances. Your remaining budget updates as real numbers replace estimates.
5. Export your plan Use PDF, Excel, or CSV when you need a copy of your plan. This helps you review the budget later or discuss it with your partner.

What this planner calculates

The planner is not only a total-cost calculator. It connects the total budget, guest count, category allocation, expense tracking, and export report so you can see how one decision changes the rest of the wedding plan.

Example: How a $40,000 wedding budget can be split

A sample budget is not a rule. It is a starting point for testing whether the largest wedding costs fit inside the amount you want to spend. The table below matches the planner's default $40,000 example so the sample numbers and calculator start from the same assumptions.

Category Example amount Why this category matters
Venue and ceremony $11,000 Often includes the first large deposit and may affect rentals, timing, and guest capacity.
Catering and drinks $13,000 Usually changes quickly when the guest count increases.
Photography $4,000 Often priced by package, coverage hours, and delivery options.
Decor and flowers $4,000 Easy to expand slowly through small extras that add up.
Entertainment $3,500 Covers music, sound, and reception atmosphere choices that vary by event style.
Attire, beauty, and accessories $3,000 Can include more than the dress or suit, such as alterations, shoes, hair, and makeup.
Transportation $1,500 May include couple transport, family transport, shuttle service, or parking-related costs.

Guest count example

In the default $40,000 example, catering and drinks are set at $13,000. At 100 guests, that equals about $130 per guest before other guest-based costs such as rentals, favors, invitations, or transportation are added.

This is why the planner shows cost per guest beside the total budget. Before choosing upgrades, test whether the guest count, venue, catering, and buffer still fit together.

Methodology and limitations

This planner uses a simple category-based budgeting method. It starts with your total budget, divides spending into common wedding categories, and updates the remaining amount as you add expenses. The examples use USD for clarity, but you can replace the sample numbers with your own currency, quotes, and local costs.

This site is a free personal planning tool by Dan. Liu / Wedding Budget Planner. It does not replace a professional wedding planner, financial adviser, or vendor quote. The purpose is to make trade-offs visible before deposits, balances, and guest-count changes become stressful.
