Taiwan wedding banquet costs in English: six hotel price lists for 2026 and 2027

DL
Written by Dan Liu
Published November 6, 2026

The six Taiwan hotels covered here each price a wedding banquet per table, then add 10%. Across the prices they published for 2026 or 2027, the lowest listed starting price is NT$15,800 per table and the highest is NT$65,800, and a "from" price is a starting tier, not a total. This guide lays out what each hotel published, how the minimum tables and minimum spend change the total, what the gifts and discounts are tied to, and what to ask before you compare any two.

Six hotels, six price lists

Only ILLUME Taipei's price comes from the hotel's own page. The other five come from Chinese-language news and magazine articles that report hotel promotions, so they show what a hotel announced on the date shown, not necessarily the price you would be quoted today. All were read on November 6, 2026. Three of the programs are for 2026 weddings (ILLUME, Taipei Garden and Tiancheng) and three are for 2027 (Lao Ye, Kaohsiung Nikko and Mandarin Oriental), so the prices are not for the same year. The Chinese articles are paraphrased here, and the terms they use are explained near the end.

ILLUME is the only one of the six whose page states guests per table, at ten. The other five do not say how many guests sit at a table in the part this guide read. Ten is the common figure, but a table of eleven or twelve changes the per-guest price, so it is worth asking before you divide anything.

What "from" means

Several of the figures are starting prices. Taipei Tiancheng's report gives "from NT$15,800" for its entry tier, with further tiers at NT$16,800, NT$18,800 and NT$20,800; the top tier adds dishes such as lobster, abalone and shark fin. Taipei Garden Hotel lists two packages: from NT$19,800 for ten or more tables, and from NT$22,800 for twenty or more tables, with a welcome cocktail reception valued at NT$12,000 added to the larger one. Kaohsiung Nikko's 2027 package starts at NT$23,999.

Taipei Lao Ye Hotel's 2027 Ming Palace project lists four prices without tying each to a menu: NT$20,800, NT$23,800, NT$26,800 and NT$30,800. Taipei Mandarin Oriental lists a plan for specific 2027 dates from NT$28,800 and a signature series from NT$32,800 to NT$65,800, again without mapping each price to a plan. In every case the practical lesson is the same: ask which tier your quote is for, what dishes and drinks that tier includes, and whether the date you want carries a different price.

Floors: tables and minimum spend

A price per table is only half the quote. Each hotel also sets a floor, and the floor decides which price you can use.

  • ILLUME Taipei: bookings can start at six tables.
  • Taipei Garden Hotel: ten or more tables for the lower package, twenty or more for the higher one. On designated weekdays, ten tables earn one free table, and the article does not list the excluded dates.
  • Taipei Tiancheng Grand Hotel: the report mentions a 20-table guarantee for the program and does not say whether it applies to every tier.
  • Taipei Lao Ye Hotel: ten tables for the early-bird terms. On weekends and holidays that fall on auspicious dates, the minimum is ten tables or a NT$230,000 minimum spend.
  • Kaohsiung Nikko Hotel: twenty tables or more.
  • Taipei Mandarin Oriental: the article gives minimum spends for its rooms rather than a table count: the Grand Ballroom, which holds up to 68 tables, has a minimum of NT$1.9 million, reduced to NT$1.52 million with a 20% discount for weddings on or before April 30, 2027. The Wenhua Hall, up to 28 tables, goes from NT$800,000 to NT$640,000.

A minimum spend is a floor on the whole banquet, not on one table. At the Mandarin Oriental figures above, NT$1.52 million is about 48 tables at NT$31,680 each, which is NT$28,800 plus 10%. The article does not say which plan applies to which room or how the minimum is measured, so a smaller banquet would need either a different room or a direct answer from the hotel. Treat that as a question to ask, not a conclusion.

The 10% and the deadline clock

Every price on this page carries "+10%." Taipei Tiancheng's report calls it a service charge and Kaohsiung Nikko's report calls it a service charge, while Taipei Lao Ye's article calls it a service fee. ILLUME, Taipei Garden and Mandarin Oriental show it without a label. Whatever it is called, it is added to the whole banquet, so it is part of the price you are comparing.

Taipei Lao Ye shows how much it can matter. Its 2027 early-bird terms waived the 10% for couples who paid the deposit by October 31, 2026 and booked at least ten tables. At ten tables, that is NT$20,800 saved at the NT$20,800 price and NT$30,800 saved at the NT$30,800 price. As of the date this guide was published, that deadline has passed, so the waiver is useful as an example of a deadline-driven offer and not as a price to plan around. Taipei Mandarin Oriental's article describes a similar clock: some of its extra gifts and discounts applied to couples who signed on its August 22 event day, and the 20% minimum-spend discount applies to weddings on or before April 30, 2027.

The pattern is that the lowest price often belongs to a date, a table count and a signing deadline at once. Write down all three next to every figure you collect, or two quotes that look similar may not be available to you on the same terms.

Gifts and thresholds: what the extras are tied to

Most of the six lists pair the per-table price with extras, and the extras are tied to a spending level, a table count or a signing date. Taipei Mandarin Oriental's article says spending NT$320,000 or more on its event day lets a couple choose one gift from six options, with more choices at higher tiers up to NT$1.52 million. Taipei Tiancheng's report says spending NT$300,000 or more earns a one-night stay with two meals at a sister hotel in Ruisui, Hualien, and Taipei Garden's says a single transaction of NT$450,000 earns a stay package at a sister hotel. Taipei Lao Ye's article lists room upgrades at 16 and 21 tables, and Kaohsiung Nikko's report ties add-ons such as a champagne tower and a wedding cake to spending thresholds it does not state.

ILLUME's page takes a different approach: it lists what comes with the weekday price, including two bottles of red wine per table, unlimited juice, a pre-wedding meal for the couple and one night in a Deluxe Suite with breakfast for two, and it does not tie them to a table count beyond the six-table start.

A simple rule keeps these offers from distorting a comparison. Give a gift a value in your budget only if you would have bought it anyway, and only if you can still use it, for example a hotel stay on a date and in a place that suits you. Count the rest as zero. Then compare the per-table totals, including the 10%, not the headline value of the gifts.

Questions to put in writing

  • How many guests sit at a table, and is a partly filled table charged in full?
  • Is this price a starting tier, and what do the higher tiers add?
  • What does the 10% cover, and is any tax added after it?
  • What are the minimum tables and the minimum spend for my date?
  • Which dates are excluded from weekday prices or from free-table offers?
  • What is the deposit, by when, and what happens to it if I cancel or change the date?
  • Are the gifts, room nights and discounts tied to a signing day or a spending threshold?

What these price lists cannot tell you

They are promotional prices that hotels published or that journalists reported, not what any couple paid. Five of the six are news or magazine reports, two were published in December 2025 and cover 2026 programs that end on December 31, 2026, and the 2027 programs may change. Hotels outside Taipei and Kaohsiung are not covered, restaurants and other venue types are not covered, and none of these figures is an average for Taiwan. This guide gives general information, not financial advice, and describes no personal experience. If you are paying from another currency, the guide on budgeting in two currencies converts NT$ banquet totals to US dollars at a dated reference rate and shows what a rate move and card fees can add.
