Quote audit

Hidden wedding costs to check before you sign

DL
Written by Dan. Liu / Wedding Budget Planner
Updated October 4, 2026

A package price can leave out required charges or costs that depend on the final guest count. Read the quote line by line before deciding it fits your budget.

Your starting checklist

  • Mark each line as included, required extra, or still uncertain.
  • Ask which subtotal the vendor uses for service charges and tax; ask whether a separate tip is expected.
  • Check travel, setup, rentals, meals, cleanup, and likely overtime.
  • Record when each amount is due, and keep money aside for unresolved costs.

How a $10,000 package reaches $14,020

This fictional USD quote applies a 20% service charge to the package and an illustrative 8% tax to the $12,000 subtotal. Meals and rentals are added afterward. These are assumptions, not rules for a real caterer's tax or fees.

Also check substitutions, rush work, weather backup, insurance, permits, and damage deposits. Ask which deposits are refundable rather than assuming every payment is a permanent cost. Keep contract notes in the worksheet or written agreement; the planner is not a contract repository.

Is a service charge the same as a tip?

For U.S. federal tax purposes, the IRS distinguishes mandatory service charges from voluntary tips. Ask the vendor what the fee covers, whether staff receive it, and whether an extra gratuity is expected. Contract wording and local treatment can differ.

Get the percentage, calculation subtotal, and conditions for any later change in writing.

Do not enter a fee inside a selected quote and again as an Actual expense. Planned category limits also do not deduct money from the Overview's uncommitted total. Leave room in the category plan for unpriced work, even if the dashboard still shows money uncommitted.

How much reserve should we keep?

Base it on the quotes still missing, variable charges, and uncertainty around guests, weather, transport, and overtime. The $40,000 starting example allocates the full amount; reduce category limits or set aside part of Other if you want a reserve.

Ask for verbal inclusions and price changes in writing. Keep the quote, contract, invoices, receipts, and agreed changes together. The FTC's recordkeeping guidance explains which documents can help if you later need to resolve a problem with a business.
