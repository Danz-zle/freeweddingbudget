Wedding Catering Budget Guide: Per-Guest Costs, Hidden Fees, and Guest-Count Examples

DL
Written by Dan. Liu / Wedding Budget Planner
Practical budgeting guide • Updated July 2026

Catering is often the wedding category that changes fastest because it is tied to guest count. A menu that looks manageable at 50 guests can become difficult at 100 or 150 guests once drinks, service charge, tax, rentals, vendor meals, and staffing are included.

Examples on this page use USD for clarity. You can adapt the same structure to your own currency, location, caterer, and vendor quotes. The goal is not to tell every couple what they must spend; it is to show how to test the catering number before it takes over the rest of the wedding budget.

Quick answer: how to budget for wedding catering

A practical catering estimate should include more than the menu price. Use this formula before comparing vendors:

Catering estimate = guest count × food/drink cost per guest + service charge + tax + gratuity + rentals + vendor meals + delivery/setup/cleanup + buffer

If a caterer gives a per-person price, ask exactly what that price includes. A lower per-guest quote can become more expensive if staffing, rentals, linens, or bar service are added later.

Example catering budgets for 50, 100, and 150 guests

Use these examples as planning scenarios, not fixed price rules. The purpose is to show how quickly catering changes when the same menu is multiplied by a larger guest list.

Guest count $85 per guest base $130 per guest base Planning note
50 guests $4,250 $6,500 Smaller events may leave more room for photography, decor, or venue upgrades.
100 guests $8,500 $13,000 This matches the default planner idea that catering can be one of the largest categories.
150 guests $12,750 $19,500 At this size, even small per-person upgrades can create large total changes.

In the default $40,000 planner example, catering and drinks are set at $13,000. That is equal to about $130 per guest for 100 guests before other guest-based costs are added. If the guest list grows to 150, the same per-guest assumption would require about $19,500, which may force reductions in venue, decor, entertainment, or buffer.

Separate the meal price from the real invoice

When a caterer gives a per-guest price, ask whether it includes staffing, service charge, tax, delivery, setup, cleanup, bar service, glassware, linens, gratuity, and vendor meals. A quote that says $85 per guest may not be the final catering cost.

Line item Example Why to track it
Meal price 100 guests × $85 = $8,500 This is usually only the base food and drink estimate.
Service charge Example 20% = $1,700 May be separate from gratuity and may apply before tax.
Tax estimate Varies by location Different areas apply different tax rules to food, drinks, service, and rentals.
Vendor meals 6 meals × $35 = $210 Often required for photographers, planners, musicians, or other long-hour vendors.
Rentals and linens Quoted separately or bundled Tables, chairs, glassware, plates, flatware, and linens can change the total.

Buffet vs plated vs food truck vs DIY

Menu style affects not only food cost, but also staffing, speed of service, rentals, cleanup, and guest experience. Compare the total setup, not only the headline food price.

Catering style Budget strength Possible hidden cost Best for
Buffet Can reduce service complexity More food quantity may be needed to avoid running out Casual or semi-formal receptions
Plated meal Portions are more controlled Higher staffing and service coordination Formal receptions and fixed seating plans
Food truck Can simplify menu decisions Queue time, parking, power, permits, and backup food plans Outdoor or relaxed weddings
DIY / family-style May lower vendor invoice Labor, food safety, serving equipment, cleanup, and stress Small weddings with reliable help

Hidden catering fees checklist

Before signing a catering agreement, review each of these possible extras and record them as separate budget lines:

  • Service charge and whether it includes gratuity.
  • Tax on food, alcohol, service, and rentals.
  • Staffing, bartender, chef, captain, or server fees.
  • Delivery, setup, cleanup, breakdown, and trash removal.
  • Tables, chairs, linens, plates, flatware, glassware, and napkins.
  • Cake cutting, corkage, coffee service, water service, and late-night snacks.
  • Vendor meals for photographers, videographers, musicians, planners, or drivers.
  • Children's meals, dietary meals, and special menu substitutions.
  • Overtime fees if the reception runs late.
  • Final guest count deadline and the cost of late changes.

How to reduce catering cost without making the wedding feel cheap

The safest savings usually come from simplifying the guest list, menu, bar package, and service style before cutting food quality too aggressively.

Cost pressure Possible adjustment Why it works
Guest count is too high Reduce the list before final catering deadline Every removed guest can reduce food, drink, rentals, and favors.
Bar package is expensive Offer beer, wine, and signature drinks instead of a full open bar Limits the highest-variance drink costs.
Menu has too many courses Choose fewer courses or simpler sides Reduces food complexity, prep time, and service needs.
Late-night snacks add up Keep one simple option or skip it if dinner is substantial Small extras can become large once multiplied by guest count.

FAQ

How do I estimate a wedding catering budget?

Start with guest count multiplied by food and drink cost per guest. Then add service charge, tax, gratuity, rentals, vendor meals, delivery, setup, cleanup, and a buffer. This gives a more realistic invoice estimate than using menu price alone.

How much should I budget for catering for 100 wedding guests?

For a simple planning example, 100 guests at $85 per guest equals $8,500 before service charge, tax, rentals, vendor meals, and other fees. At $130 per guest, the base becomes $13,000 before extras. Use the calculator to test whether that fits your total budget.

What catering costs are easy to forget?

Common forgotten costs include service charge, tax, gratuity, bartender fees, staffing, delivery, setup, cleanup, rentals, linens, glassware, cake cutting, corkage, coffee service, vendor meals, and overtime.

Should I compare caterers by per-guest price?

No. Compare the full quote. One caterer may include staffing, rentals, and setup while another lists those separately. The lower per-guest price is not always the lower final invoice.
