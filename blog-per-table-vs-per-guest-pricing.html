Per-table or per-guest wedding pricing: how to compare quotes in either format

DL
Written by Dan Liu
Published November 6, 2026

A per-table quote and a per-guest quote are not the same kind of number, and the headline price of each leaves something out. To compare them fairly, turn every quote into one total for your own guest count, including every compulsory fee and percentage charge, then divide by the guests who will attend. Four published 2026 price lists, from Taipei, Singapore, a venue priced in pounds and a venue priced in US dollars, show how far the headline can sit from that total.

The short answer

  • A per-table price divided by the seats at the table gives a per-guest figure, but the total rises in whole tables. A guest count that leaves a table partly empty costs the same as a full table unless the venue says otherwise.
  • A per-guest price scales one guest at a time, but it often sits beside a separate venue or site fee and a minimum guest count, so the all-in cost per guest is higher than the headline.
  • A percentage added after the price, such as service charge and then tax, is applied to the whole of the above. Add it last, after every fixed fee.

The examples below use list prices the venues published, not what any couple paid. Each figure is in the venue's own currency and nothing has been converted between currencies.

A couple expecting 120 guests would have to ask whether a smaller room or a different package applies, because the published price does not cover that case.

Per-guest pricing: the headline leaves out the room

The other two venues price per guest, and in both the venue itself is charged separately. Falcon Manor's 2026 to 2028 pricing sheet lists per-person packages: Classic is £105 in 2026 (Deluxe £120, Exquisite £140), rising to £115 and £125 in 2027 and 2028. Venue hire is added on top and depends on the day and season: Sunday to Thursday is £500 from October to March and £1,250 from April to September, and Saturday is £1,750 or £3,000. Minimums are 50 guests from Sunday to Friday and 70 on Saturday. A Garden Room ceremony is £750 in 2026, and the packages must include all 16 bedrooms at £150 to £305 depending on room type and year. The sheet does not say which season the per-person prices assume, nor whether a service charge or tax applies.

A like-for-like method for any two quotes

  1. Name the pricing unit. Is it per table (and how many seats), per guest, or a flat fee plus a per-guest amount?
  2. Fix one scenario. Use the same guest count, day type and season for every quote. For a per-table quote, round up to whole tables.
  3. Add everything compulsory. Include venue or site fees, room blocks, ceremony fees, minimum guests or minimum spend, and anything the venue says must be bought from it, such as alcohol.
  4. Apply percentage charges last. Add service charge to the subtotal, then taxes where the quote says they follow. If a rate is missing, mark the total as a minimum.
  5. Divide by attending guests. Compare per-guest totals only after steps one to four, and write down what each quote excludes.

As a formula: total = (rate × units + fixed fees) × (1 + percentage charges), and per guest = total ÷ guests. Once you have a like-for-like total for each venue, you can enter it as a quote in the Planner's Vendors section so the comparison sits beside the rest of your budget.

Questions to put in writing before you compare

  • Is the price per table of ten, or per table as seated, and is a partly filled table charged in full?
  • What are the minimum tables or guests, and what happens if fewer people attend?
  • What does the percentage on top cover, and are taxes added after it?
  • Do weekend, eve-of-holiday and seasonal dates have different prices?
  • Are drinks, the ceremony, a tasting, parking and rooms inside the price or extra?
  • Until what date is this price valid, and can the venue change it?

What these examples cannot tell you

They are published list prices, not negotiated or paid prices. Venues often change packages without notice, and the Amara, Falcon Manor and Sun Mountain documents carry no printed document date, so treat them as examples of the format and confirm any figure with the venue. ILLUME's page shows it was last modified on August 19, 2026, but it gives no end date for the offer and no weekend price. This guide describes no personal quotes or events, and it is general information, not financial advice.
