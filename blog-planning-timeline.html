Dates and payments

Wedding planning timeline and payment calendar

DL
Written by Dan. Liu / Wedding Budget Planner
Updated October 4, 2026

Booking dates are only part of the plan. Write down when money leaves your account, when guest numbers become final, and who needs to make each decision.

Your starting checklist

  • Start with your spending ceiling, rough guest count, date, and location.
  • Read each contract for deposits, balances, cancellation terms, and change deadlines.
  • Put payment dates beside expected savings so you can see a cash shortfall early.
  • Keep guest guarantees, setup deadlines, and local paperwork requirements in your calendar.

A $35,000 booking plan still has $27,125 to pay

These fictional USD contracts require $7,875 at booking. That leaves $27,125, or 77.5% of the total, for later payments. A manageable deposit does not make the remaining bill smaller.

Use it in your planner

  1. Save each vendor quote in Vendors and select the commitment you intend to book.
  2. In Payments, choose that vendor and enter each deposit, installment, or balance with its amount and actual due date.
  3. Mark a payment paid only after paying it. Keep the receipt, and check that the scheduled total agrees with the contract.

The planner shows payment status when you open it. Use your own calendar for reminders, task owners, contract changes, and deadlines that are not payments.

How should we use a 12-month timeline?

Use this as an order of decisions, not a rule about when every wedding must be booked. Shorter engagements, courthouse ceremonies, destination trips, and multi-day events need different timing.

What changes when RSVPs arrive?

At roughly 3 to 2 months, compare replies with vendor guarantee deadlines. Your contract may require a final number sooner or later.

Agree who handles each decision and where the relevant documents live. Keep quotes, contracts, invoices, receipts, and approved changes. The FTC's guidance on resolving problems with businesses explains why those records help.

What should we check in the final month and week?

Match final invoices to the agreed scope and guest count. Confirm payment recipients through a trusted contact, keep receipts, and share arrival, setup, teardown, and emergency contact details with the people who need them.

Review the venue's weather and disruption plan, including the decision-maker and safe shelter or evacuation arrangements. Use official local alerts. In the U.S., the FEMA app is one option for weather and emergency alerts.

Do not enter a deposit again as an Actual expense if it is already a payment within a selected commitment. Reports gives you a review copy; My Data gives you a backup for restoring the planner.
