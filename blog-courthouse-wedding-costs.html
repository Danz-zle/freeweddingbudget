Courthouse and micro weddings

Courthouse wedding costs: the legal fees line by line, from seven US pages

DL
Written by Dan Liu
Published November 6, 2026

In seven county and court pages read on November 6, 2026, the listed legal fees for a courthouse wedding run from $60 for a license alone to $171 for a license plus a ceremony fee, and $207 where a witness fee is also listed. Where you apply and which small charges the county lists decide the figure. Everything else, from the photographer to a small meal, has to come from your own quotes.

The legal fees on seven pages

The table lists what each page states. Where a page lists no ceremony fee, the "license plus ceremony or certificate charge" column is only the license, not a promise that the ceremony is free. The pages are county or court sites, not summaries, and most show no date of their own. San Luis Obispo's page is headed as effective July 1, 2026, Goodhue County's is a finance memo dated January 20, 2026, and Cleveland's is a court news release dated January 12, 2026.

Among the five jurisdictions that list both a license and a second charge, the listed total runs from $80 (Cuyahoga and Cleveland, for a one-day city hall event) to $171 (San Luis Obispo), and $110 in Wake County, North Carolina, where the magistrate fee is $50 in exact cash. The two places that list only a license, Goodhue and Cook, show how much a single page can leave out: a ceremony fee may exist elsewhere on the same office's site.

San Luis Obispo's page is useful because it shows both the old and the new fee. Its public license rose from $104 to $105, solemnization from $58 to $66, the rooftop wedding ceremony from $70 to $80 and the civil ceremony witness fee from $31 to $36 on July 1, 2026. Small county fee changes are routine, so a figure copied from an older guide, including this one, should be checked against the issuing office's current page before you plan around it.

What moves the bill

The same pages show several small choices that change the total. None is large on its own, but they decide whether you arrive with the right amount in the right form of payment.

Payment form is its own cost of arriving unprepared. Cleveland's event required cash at both offices, and Wake County's magistrate fee is $50 in exact change. Sacramento's page does not state payment methods, and Pima County, Arizona, points to a separate fee schedule that this guide did not read, so it is left out of the table. Check the payment methods before the day.

Timing matters as well. Wake County's page says the ceremony must take place in North Carolina within 60 days of getting the license. Cook County's page says both partners must appear together at one of its four clerk locations. Cleveland's event required the license to be collected by 3:00 p.m. on the Friday before. A missed deadline can mean starting again, so ask the office what happens.

The lines no fee page can price

A courthouse wedding is not only the legal fee. A small photo session, an outfit, flowers, a meal afterwards and the trip all cost money, and none of them is set by the county. If you are adding a small celebration, the scale of the event matters. A CNBC report published July 6, 2024 and updated July 8, 2024 cites The Wedding Report as saying that in 2023, weddings with 25 to 50 guests were about 15% of the market and those with fewer than 25 guests were about 2%. The article defines a micro wedding as roughly 50 guests at most. It gives no average cost for micro weddings, so treat it as context about size, not a price. For how guest count changes catering and venue lines, see the guide on 50 vs 100 guests.

Build your own line-by-line total

  1. Find the issuing office. Use the county clerk, recorder or register of deeds page for the county where you will apply, not a third-party summary.
  2. Write down every charge it lists for a license, a ceremony, a witness, a certificate and any after-hours appointment, with the date shown on the page.
  3. Note the payment form and the deadlines, such as a validity window or a cutoff time.
  4. Add your own quoted lines from the table above, with each quote in writing.
  5. Add a small reserve for a fee that changes or a document you need again.

Once you have the total, you can enter it in the Planner's Budget section as your ceremony line.

What these pages cannot tell you

Seven jurisdictions in five states are not a national average, and a county you are considering may differ. Most pages carry no date of their own, so a fee may already have changed. Several pages list a license fee but no ceremony fee, which can mean either that none exists or that it sits on another page. This guide gives general information, not legal advice, and describes no personal experience.
