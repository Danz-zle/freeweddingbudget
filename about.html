About the project

About Wedding Budget Planner

I am Dan Liu, an independent site builder. I built Wedding Budget Planner as a project to make the full cost of vendor quotes easier to compare.

I have been married for over ten years, but I am not a wedding professional, financial adviser, lawyer, venue operator or vendor marketplace. Wedding Budget Planner does not provide professional wedding, financial or legal advice.

The planner is free and does not require an account. The site is free to use and may show Google advertising; the Privacy Policy explains how. The information entered into it is stored only in the visitor's browser, not on a server.

Why I built it

Vendor quotes may present costs in different ways. One quote may place required charges inside the package price, while another may list them separately. Travel, delivery, rentals, overtime and tax may also affect the final total.

I built Wedding Budget Planner to give these costs a consistent structure. Visitors can enter figures from their own quotes and compare the complete amounts instead of looking only at the advertised package prices.

The site does not sell vendor rankings or placement. Its examples do not promote any venue, caterer, photographer or retailer.

What the planner stores

Wedding Budget Planner saves planning data only in the browser being used. It does not require visitors to register or create an account.

Because the data stays in the browser, a plan does not automatically move to another browser or device. The backup and restore function lets visitors download a copy of their planning record and restore it in another compatible browser session.

How the guides are written

The guides use fictional examples to explain budgeting methods. Each fictional example is identified as fictional so that it is not mistaken for a real quote or a universal price.

When a guide includes figures from a survey, it links to the organization that published those figures. Readers can follow the link to review the source's complete methodology, definitions and limitations.

Several guides now use real published sources. The courthouse, Taiwan banquet, Singapore banquet and per-table pricing guides compare public fee pages and hotel price lists, and each one links to the source and gives the date I read it. Where a source has limits, such as an undated page, a price reported by a news site, or an assumption of mine, the guide says so. Fictional examples remain labeled as fictional, so readers can tell them apart from published information.
